## 3. Assumptions

- A1 Rates: OpenAI's published standard-tier rates for gpt-6-luna (checked 2026-10-24). Our calls go through Portkey to an Azure OpenAI deployment; the course account's actual billing (rates, markups, credits) is unknown.
- A2 Caching: cached-read and cache-write token counts were never recorded, so the point estimate bills all input at the uncached rate. Bounds: for prompts of at least 1,024 tokens (the guide's minimum for GPT-5.6 and later), the low bound bills all input as cached reads (0.1x) and the high bound as 30-minute cache writes (1.25x); shorter prompts cannot be cached and use the uncached rate in all three figures.
- A3 Images: photo-analysis input_tokens are assumed to include the image tokens (the guide does not say for gpt-6-luna). Output tokens are assumed to include any reasoning tokens (not recorded separately).
- A4 Context: every recorded call is far below the 272,000-token long-context threshold, so short-context rates apply.
- A5 Repeats: identical prompts that returned identical outputs and token counts may have been served from a Portkey cache; whether those were billed is unknown, so they are counted as billed.

## 8. Key findings

- Every workflow costs well under a tenth of a cent per call at published rates: from $0.000082 (safety screen) to $0.000539 (photo analysis).
- The P31 safety screen adds 31% to the cost of extracting one report ($0.000265 → $0.000346).
- Output is billed 5× input. Share of the point cost from output tokens: warehouse photo analysis 65%, shelter report extraction 63%, safety screening (p31+) 50%, photo follow-up question 20%, chat / tool calling 46%.
- Caching matters only above 1,024 tokens per request: extraction (~976 input tokens) and the screen (~410) are below it, so caching cannot change their cost; chat and photo calls are above it, which is why their bounds are wide.
- Chat figures are the weakest data (observed, not saved) and also the most variable: input grows with tool outputs and conversation history.
- 6 of 8 photo analyses and 3 of 17 extractions exactly repeat earlier calls; whether Portkey served and billed them is unknown.

8. Measure latency and peak concurrency (requests per minute) to check rate limits at 100 and 10,000 users.
9. Decide the service tier (standard vs batch/flex) per workflow; batch/flex halves the published rates but adds delay.
